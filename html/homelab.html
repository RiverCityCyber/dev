Principles of Homelabbing

My princicples to running and maintaining a homelab within a budget are as follows:

  1. Least power needed
  2. Least access needed
  3. Full logging
  4. Automate where possible
  5. Document every step
  6. Smallest footprint possible
These principles exist to simplify and secure my homelab. I want to keep the power consumption and risk low, while still providing the services I enjoy from my homelab. Therefore, good cybersecurity practices and use of tools such as Wake on Lan (WOL) are critical. VPNs, PKI, MFA, IPS, and other wonderful acronyms will allow homelab user experience to stay smooth and security to stay tight.

Hardware

Following principle #1, my homelab should have as few servers running as possible. Therefore, I have listed what hardware I am running, the approximate power draw, what services it runs, and the approximate resource utilization. Resource utilization is especially useful as it allows us to see if we are being constrained, or if we are properly utilizing what we have running. Ideally, we would want 80-90% utilization of RAM at all times and 90% utilization of CPU during peak loads.

The Always on PC

The job of this server is to run any services that must always be on. To conserve resources, I do not have a hypervisor running, but am using docker containers or running as a service on Ubuntu Server. I chose Ubuntu server for the simplicity, ease of use, reliability, and widespread support. Just about any home service you'd like to run can run on Ubuntu server.

  • CPU: Intel Core i5-7500 3.4 GHz Quad-Core Processor
  • Motherboard: Asus PRIME B250M-C-CSM Micro ATX LGA1151
  • RAM: 16GB DDR4-2400 CL 17
  • Storage: 256GB 2.5" SATA SSD

Note: I would advise for any server that you have running 24/7 at home to stay away from HDDs when storage is under 2TB. M.2 drives are ideal, but even SSDs will save you a lot on power cost. For video storage, HDDs are ideal simply due to their better cost per byte of storage.
